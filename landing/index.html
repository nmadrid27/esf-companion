What is AI drift?

Drift is what happens when you accept AI output without evaluating it against what you actually think. It’s not dramatic. It’s incremental: one accepted suggestion, one skipped review, one deadline that pushed you to just use the draft.

Most AI tools have no mechanism to prevent it. ESF Companion does: you write a Position Statement before AI enters your work. Every AI suggestion gets measured against it. When the work starts drifting from your position, the Companion asks you about it. You decide what to do. The Companion never decides for you.

How it works

Five phases. The first two are yours alone — no AI.

1

Inquire

Human only

Understand the problem without AI. What is this actually asking? What do you already know? What are your assumptions?

2

Position ✍️

Human only

Write your Position Statement — without AI. 200–400 words. What is your approach? What matters most? What will you not compromise?

AI cannot enter until your Position Statement exists. This is not a rule — it is the mechanism that keeps your work yours. No Position Statement, no AI assistance.

3

Explore

AI-assisted

Bring AI in to challenge your position, not produce your work. Ask it to generate alternatives, challenge your assumptions, find relevant examples. Evaluate everything against your Position Statement.

4

Make

AI-assisted

Build the work with AI assistance. Check each section against your Position Statement. Apply the Five Questions at major decision points. Document what you kept, revised, and rejected — and why.

5

Reflect

Human only

Compare your final work to your Position Statement. What changed? What held? Write your honest disclosure. AI may check completeness and readability after you draft it.

The Five Questions

Run these before accepting AI output, before submitting, before publishing.

01

Can I defend this?

Could I explain and justify every claim, choice, and structure to a knowledgeable audience?

02

Is this mine?

Did I direct this work, or did I accept the AI’s framing without evaluating it against my own position?

03

Did I verify?

Have I checked facts, sources, code behavior, or logical consistency independently of what the AI told me?

04

Would I teach this?

Do I understand this well enough to explain it to someone else and answer their questions?

05

Is my disclosure honest?

Does my AI Use Log accurately describe what I did and what AI did?

All five pass? Proceed.
One fails? Stop and address it.
