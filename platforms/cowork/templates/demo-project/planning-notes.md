# Planning notes — cartography series

(rough, written in one sitting before any AI involvement)

Working title: *Cartographies of Absence*. Maybe *Erasure Atlas*. Probably the first one.

The thing I keep coming back to is that "decolonial mapping" as a category has gotten flat. People publish maps with Indigenous place names overlaid on settler grids and call it done. That is not what I want to make. The overlay reads as a correction, like a grammar fix. What I want is for the act of naming to be the primary gesture, not a footnote on the colonial original.

Things I know:
- Five maps. The number matters. Less than three reads as a tease, more than seven loses the argument.
- The maps need to feel like they are actively refusing the colonial frame, not just illustrating its existence.
- Wall text is dangerous. If I over-explain, the work becomes didactic. If I under-explain, the gallery audience does not have the context to read the work.
- Source data is going to be a real problem. I have been to the consultation pages of two of the local nations and they are clear that name extraction without ongoing relationship is not okay. I cannot pretend I have a relationship I do not have. The realistic version of this project may have to use names from communities I am already in conversation with, and acknowledge the gap.

What is non-negotiable:
- Every map must name what has been erased, by name. No silhouettes, no shaded regions, no abstractions of the loss. The point of the work falls apart if the names are not present.
- The series is one argument, not five objects. If a single map could stand alone, it has not done its job.

What I am uncertain about:
- Whether the wall text should speak in first person or third. First person makes me the author; third person makes the work the author. I am not sure which is right yet.
- Whether to include the colonial names at all. Including them is the safer crit move (it shows the contrast). Excluding them is the more honest move to the work's own argument. I am leaning toward exclusion.
- Whether the print size is right. 24x36 feels assertive but I have not tested it on a wall yet.

Questions I want answered before crit:
- Has someone already done the version of this project that I am imagining? Where?
- What would the work need to do for the panel to read it as an argument and not a portfolio piece?
- Where am I going to get tripped up?
